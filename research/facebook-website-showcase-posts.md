# Research: premium Facebook/Instagram website showcase for JetCarsBelize

Research date: 2026-09-14

## Bottom line

Do not lead with “here are screenshots of a website we built.” Lead with a sharp business story: **a Belize adventure business now lets visitors move from curiosity to a booking request without WhatsApp back-and-forth.** The website is the evidence, not the subject.

The strongest primary-source signal is to make the hero asset a native vertical Reel, then support it with a deliberately art-directed carousel. Meta reports that Reels ads made as 9:16 video, with audio and key messages inside the safe zone, produced **34.5% lower cost per result than image ads** in a meta-analysis of 15 split tests (99.9% confidence). That does not guarantee this project’s result, but it is real evidence for choosing motion over a screenshot dump.

## Diagnosis of the current post

The current asset asks a viewer to inspect a full-page desktop screenshot and a narrow booking screenshot at the same time. The text is partly cropped, the product UI is too small to read, and the split has no deliberate focal point. It shows that pages exist but does not dramatize what changed for the business or customer.

Specific problems:

- The jet-car photo, headline, pricing and booking form all compete for attention.
- The screenshots are documentary rather than art-directed; there is no hook, reveal, sequence or point of view.
- Showing the entire page reduces the most valuable interaction—the booking flow—to tiny form fields.
- “A recent website we built” centers the maker. A prospective client cares first about the business problem, customer experience and commercial value.
- The generic “Need a better website?” close does not give a Belize business owner a specific reason to message now.

## Recommended campaign idea: **From splash to booked**

### 1. Hero Reel (primary asset)

Create a 12–18 second, 9:16 film. It should feel like a compact tourism trailer crossed with a product reveal.

Suggested beat sheet:

1. **0.0–1.2 sec — pattern interrupt:** real close-up footage of the lime jet car cutting through blue water; hard cut on spray. On-screen line: “A booking experience built for the water.”
2. **1.2–3.5 sec — brand reveal:** the waterline wipes into the JetCarsBelize home page. Do not place it in a generic laptop mockup; let the interface occupy the frame.
3. **3.5–8.5 sec — proof through motion:** three fast, legible screen-recording moments: choose the ride → choose an available time → send the booking request. Use oversized crops so each tap reads instantly.
4. **8.5–12.5 sec — connect digital to reality:** alternate site action with real jet-car footage: selection / engine start, time / rider pushing off, booking confirmation / wake trail.
5. **12.5–16 sec — client-acquisition close:** “Your website should move customers forward.” Then: “Built in Belize for JetCarsBelize” and one CTA, “Message ‘WEBSITE’ on WhatsApp.”

Production notes:

- Use real JetCarsBelize footage and native screen recordings. The originality is part of the pitch.
- Build sound around engine ignition, water impact and one restrained music bed. Burn in minimal captions so the idea works muted.
- Keep all critical copy and the booking UI inside Reels safe zones.
- Avoid phone/laptop mockup packs, floating glass cards, neon gradients, UI flying through 3D space, or a trendy transition unrelated to water. Those cues make agency work look interchangeable.
- Make the cover a designed poster, not a random video frame.

### 2. Editorial carousel (supporting asset)

Use five 4:5 cards. Each card should make one point and work as a poster on its own.

1. **Cover:** full-bleed jet-car crop with a controlled dark sea overlay. Large line: “From splash to booked.” Small line: “A new digital booking experience for JetCarsBelize.”
2. **The friction:** “Customers want the price, date and time before they message.” Show a tasteful fragment of a chat thread dissolving into the clean booking UI; use no fabricated customer quotes.
3. **The journey:** three oversized steps across the composition: `01 Choose a ride` / `02 Pick a time` / `03 Request the booking`.
4. **The proof:** the single strongest mobile booking screen paired with one desktop hero crop. Do not show full-page screenshots. Caption: “Designed for the phone in your hand—and the trip already in your head.”
5. **The prospect CTA:** “Your website can do more than list your services.” Subline: “If customers still need to ask every basic question, let’s fix the path.” CTA: “Message ‘WEBSITE’ on WhatsApp.”

### Visual direction

- Palette: Atlantic/Caribbean teal, deep navy-black, sea-glass green and warm sand/ivory—sampled from the actual JetCarsBelize world.
- Typography: one expressive editorial serif for the campaign line and one disciplined sans for product/booking details. Use large type and wide breathing room.
- Texture: sun glare, water caustics, wake lines and a subtle printed-tourism grain. Use them as a system, not decoration.
- Composition: asymmetrical editorial crops and full-bleed media; one dominant idea per frame.
- Branding: JetCarsBelize remains the hero. The developer credit is a small, confident signature, not a competing logo lockup.

## Cover/hook variants worth testing

Keep the body creative fixed and vary only the packaging:

- **Outcome hook:** “From splash to booked.”
- **Pain hook:** “Still answering every price and availability question by message?”
- **Identity hook:** “A Belize website should not look like a template.”
- **Experience hook:** “We made booking the adventure feel like part of the adventure.”

The premium choice is the first or fourth. The second will likely be more direct-response oriented; the third is stronger for brand positioning but more confrontational.

## Measurement plan

Define success as qualified client conversations, not reactions.

- Organic: reach among non-followers, three-second views, average watch time/completion, saves, shares, profile visits, outbound WhatsApp taps and qualified DMs.
- If paid promotion is later approved: cost per qualified WhatsApp conversation or lead, not cost per like.
- Use distinct links/UTM parameters for the Reel and carousel if linking to a portfolio page.
- Test one variable at a time: cover/hook first, then Reel versus carousel. Facebook’s Reels A/B tool can test thumbnails/captions where available.
- Record baseline values and evaluate creative after enough delivery; do not declare a winner from a handful of local impressions.

## Recommendation in one sentence

Build a **water-driven, 12–18 second vertical case-study film** and a matching **five-card editorial story** that show how the site removes booking friction; sell the business transformation, use the interface as proof, and close with one trackable WhatsApp keyword.
